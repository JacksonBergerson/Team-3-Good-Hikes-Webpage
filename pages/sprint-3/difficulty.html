Our Difficulty Rating System

Our difficulty system is based off of the Petzoldt Energy Mile theory which, in the simplest terms for our purposes, means that for each 500ft you ascend, you are exerting yourself the same amount as if you walked one mile on flat ground. Our system is on a 1-10 scale for easy comprehension and considers several factors. Under the hood is an effort-based points system, with a mile and 500ft of ascension each being worth 1 effort point, with different point thresholds for where the hikes land on the 1-10 scale.
For example, a 5/10 difficulty hike requires an effort score of 9 or 10 points respectively, which could correlate to a hike like Skyline Loop, which has a length around 6 miles and an elevation gain of around 1,500ft. Additionally, trail terrain and current weather conditions multiply the effort score.

Current weather data comes from OpenWeatherMap using each hike's trailhead coordinates. Rain, snow, high wind, extreme temperatures, and high humidity increase the difficulty multiplier, up to 2x. Temperature is measured in Fahrenheit and wind speed in miles per hour.
